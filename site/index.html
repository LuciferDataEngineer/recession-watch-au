What this means

The estimate is +0.2% for Sep 2026 and +0.1% for Dec 2026. The chance Sep 2026 shrinks is 12%. The chance Dec 2026 shrinks is 41%, because that estimate sits close to zero. The chance both shrink is 5%. Per person, the estimates are −0.1% and −0.3%, and the chance both of those are negative is 67%. A second check, using whether similar quarters in the past actually shrank, puts the chance the next GDP print is negative at only 1%. Westpac’s published note of 20 November put September-quarter growth near 0.5%, the chance of a shrinking December quarter at 30%, and the chance of two shrinking quarters at 5 to 6%. Those are their figures, not this page’s.

Official September-quarter GDP is due 2 Dec 2026, 11:30 am AEDT. Until then, treat the amber bars as an estimate with a wide range, not as a fact.

The unemployment rate is the early warning that has already moved

The gap between long and short bond yields is still positive

What this means

Unemployment is +0.6 points above its low of the past year. Longer-term government bond yields are still +0.3 points above shorter-term yields, so the bond market is not signalling a recession the way an inverted gap would. Inflation at 4.3% is why the cash rate is 4.85% even as hiring falls. The September-quarter national accounts, out on 2 December, are the next number that can settle the argument.
